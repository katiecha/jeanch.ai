# Monument Valley Style Guide

## Overview

Monument Valley's visual language can be described as:

**Minimalist Geometric Surrealism**

A fusion of:

* Architectural design
* Impossible geometry
* Minimalist visual systems
* Paper-sculpture aesthetics
* Calm, contemplative interaction design

The experience feels less like a traditional digital product and more like a handcrafted architectural model brought to life.

---

# Core Design Principles

## 1. Geometry First

The geometry itself becomes the visual language.

### Characteristics

* Cubes
* Arches
* Stairs
* Platforms
* Bridges
* Cylinders
* Towers

Avoid:

* Organic shapes
* Decorative flourishes
* Complex illustrations
* Unnecessary visual detail

### Feeling

The world should feel constructed rather than drawn.

---

## 2. Extreme Visual Reduction

Every visual element must justify its existence.

Remove:

* Texture noise
* Realistic materials
* Excessive shadows
* Surface imperfections
* Decorative effects

Favor:

* Flat surfaces
* Clean edges
* Simple gradients
* Large areas of uninterrupted color

### Rule

If a detail does not improve clarity, remove it.

---

## 3. Clarity Through Hierarchy

Every screen should have an obvious focal point.

### Characteristics

* One primary object
* One primary action
* Minimal visual competition
* Strong contrast between foreground and background

### Goal

Immediate comprehension.

Nothing should compete for attention.

---

## 4. Soft Depth

Depth should come from composition rather than realism.

Create depth using:

* Scale
* Layering
* Overlap
* Perspective
* Color separation

Avoid:

* Heavy shadows
* Photorealistic rendering
* Complex lighting systems

### Inspiration

Architectural models.

Museum installations.

Physical maquettes.

---

## 5. Impossible Geometry

Monument Valley's defining characteristic.

Spaces appear logical while subtly violating expectations.

### Characteristics

* Forced perspective
* Visual ambiguity
* Hidden relationships
* Unexpected alignments
* Spatial illusions

### Feeling

The environment should feel familiar at first glance and impossible upon closer inspection.

---

## 6. Physical Yet Abstract

Objects should feel tangible without becoming realistic.

Users should feel as though they could:

* Hold them
* Rotate them
* Place them on a desk

Yet the forms remain abstract and symbolic.

### Inspiration

* Paper sculptures
* Toy architecture
* Abstract monuments
* Conceptual models

---

## 7. Restrained Color Systems

Color is used sparingly and intentionally.

### Palette Structure

* 1–2 dominant colors
* 1 supporting color
* 1 accent color

### Characteristics

* Muted
* Soft
* Harmonious
* Curated

Avoid:

* Highly saturated palettes
* Large numbers of colors
* Strong visual noise

### Example Palettes

#### Palette A

* Dusty Pink
* Warm Cream
* Deep Teal

#### Palette B

* Sage Green
* Soft Orange
* Charcoal

#### Palette C

* Lunar Beige
* Oxide Red
* Dark Slate

---

## 8. Deliberate Motion

Motion should communicate structure.

### Characteristics

* Slow
* Smooth
* Predictable
* Intentional

Animations should feel architectural.

Movement reveals form.

Movement reveals relationships.

Movement reveals understanding.

Avoid:

* Fast transitions
* Bouncy effects
* Attention-seeking animations

### Goal

Comprehension, not excitement.

---

# Typography

Typography should remain restrained and elegant.

### Characteristics

* Large, confident headings
* Generous spacing
* Limited font usage
* Strong readability

### Feeling

A balance between architecture and poetry.

Text should feel carved into the environment rather than layered on top of it.

---

# Whitespace

Whitespace is a design material.

Not empty space.

### Principles

* Large margins
* Few elements per screen
* Breathing room around content
* Strong alignment

### Feeling

Calm.

Open.

Intentional.

---

# Emotional Targets

The interface should feel:

* Calm
* Thoughtful
* Architectural
* Curious
* Precise
* Dreamlike
* Timeless
* Meditative

Avoid:

* Corporate
* Aggressive
* Trend-driven
* Overstimulating
* Visually crowded
* Hyper-realistic

---

# Visual References

Think:

* Architectural models
* M. C. Escher sketches
* Modernist monuments
* Museum installations
* Paper sculptures
* Desert temples
* Minimalist stage design

Not:

* Corporate SaaS dashboards
* Cyberpunk interfaces
* Photorealistic environments
* Maximalist game worlds
* Traditional fantasy settings

---

# Design Summary

Monument Valley succeeds because it combines:

* Geometric simplicity
* Architectural composition
* Impossible spatial logic
* Restrained color palettes
* Deliberate motion
* Extreme visual clarity

The result is a world that feels simultaneously physical and impossible, minimal and memorable, playful and contemplative.
